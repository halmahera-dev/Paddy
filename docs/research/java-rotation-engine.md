# Rotation comparison engine for a Java pilot

**Checked:** 26 September 2026. **Decision question:** What is the smallest defensible way to compare two rice and secondary-crop sequences for a district or farming zone? This note uses primary sources from FAO, NASA, and Indonesia's Ministry of Agriculture.

## Recommendation

Use a **transparent calendar and water-screening baseline first**. It can reject plans that do not fit the local season, known irrigation access, or crop duration, then show each surviving plan's rain exposure and estimated crop water demand. Keep each rule, input, and source visible. For the pilot, compare this baseline with **FAO AquaCrop 7.3** for representative field types, after local calibration and independent validation. AquaCrop is a good second layer for water and yield *scenarios*, but its output is not observed yield or measured soil improvement. This is a proposed engineering sequence, not a validated result. [FAO AquaCrop overview](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/en) · [FAO input requirements](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/input-requirements/) · [Kementan KATAM](https://repository.pertanian.go.id/handle/123456789/22624)

The pilot should start with **paddy rice, maize, and soybean** as candidate crops, subject to local agronomist review. Kementan's KATAM already advises on rice, maize, and soybean planting patterns and time at subdistrict level. The service must compare against KATAM and explain any added value; it must not claim to have invented rotation guidance. [Kementan KATAM description](https://repository.pertanian.go.id/handle/123456789/22624) · [Kementan study of rice rotations](https://repository.pertanian.go.id/handle/123456789/8163)

## Two engine choices

| | Rule and water baseline | FAO AquaCrop 7.3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calculation | Check crop calendar, crop duration, field type, local irrigation availability, and sequence rules approved by an agronomist. Estimate reference evapotranspiration (ETo) and stage-specific crop evapotranspiration with FAO-56 crop coefficients; compare with effective rain by season. Keep a separate paddy flooding and percolation allowance where local data support it. [FAO-56](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/X0490E00.htm) · [FAO AQUASTAT irrigation method](https://www.fao.org/aquastat/en/data-analysis/irrig-water-use/irrig-water-requirement/) | Run daily crop and soil water simulations for each sequence, using weather, crop cultivar, soil profile, irrigation schedule, and field management. FAO provides crop parameter sets for rice, maize, and soybean. Multiple runs can carry soil water and salinity from one crop into the next when the soil profile stays the same. [FAO input requirements](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/input-requirements/) · [FAO AquaCrop 7.3 annexes](https://www.fao.org/docs/aquacroplibraries/default-document-library/annexesaquacropv73.pdf) · [FAO 7.3 user guide, sections 2.17 and 2.19](https://www.fao.org/docs/aquacroplibraries/default-document-library/chapter2aquacropv73.pdf) |
| Suitable output | Feasible or infeasible plan with reasons; planting windows; crop-season rain and heat exposure; **estimated** water demand and rainfall gap; data completeness. It cannot predict yield response or soil improvement. [FAO-56](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490E/X0490E00.htm) | Simulated canopy, transpiration, soil water, biomass, yield, irrigation demand, and water productivity under the entered assumptions. It can compare water stress scenarios, but it is a point model of a uniform field, not a district hydrology or irrigation network model. [FAO calculation scheme](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/calculation-scheme/) · [FAO model limits](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/en) |
| Data burden | Local calendar, crop duration, irrigation access, rain and weather, at least basic soil water-holding class. A result can stay qualitative if those inputs are weak. | Daily minimum and maximum temperature, rain, ETo, crop and cultivar traits, soil profile and initial soil water, groundwater where relevant, irrigation events, and management. Needs observed canopy, biomass or yield, soil water, and actual water applications to calibrate and test. [FAO input requirements](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/input-requirements/) · [FAO calibration guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/i2800e/i2800e.pdf) |
| Main risk | Water gap is a screening estimate, especially for bunded rice. It does not account for actual canal delivery, field losses, or farmers' choices. [FAO AQUASTAT method](https://www.fao.org/aquastat/en/data-analysis/irrig-water-use/irrig-water-requirement/) | Precise-looking yield numbers without local calibration. The model treats field conditions as uniform and considers vertical water fluxes; a district mean run is not a verified result for every field. [FAO model limits](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/en) |

**Technical distinction:** Crop evapotranspiration is water consumed by evaporation and transpiration. Irrigation demand also depends on effective rainfall, stored soil water, and, for paddy, water for flooding and field preparation. Neither quantity is the same as actual water delivered or withdrawn from an irrigation system. FAO explicitly adds a paddy flooding allowance in its broad irrigation-demand method. That allowance must be checked locally before use in this pilot. [FAO AQUASTAT method](https://www.fao.org/aquastat/en/data-analysis/irrig-water-use/irrig-water-requirement/)

## Inputs and scale

For each representative **farming zone**, record rainfed or irrigated field type, season calendar, present and past crop sequences, actual crop varieties and dates, soil texture and water-holding properties, drainage or bund conditions, irrigation source and deliveries, and local agronomic constraints. Preserve observed values and their provenance. Use a zone's representative field types for runs, then report how much known land each type represents; do not present one model point as a measured result for every plot. AquaCrop itself assumes one uniform field per simulation. [FAO model limits](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/en) · [FAO input requirements](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/input-requirements/)

NASA can supply **weather context, not local management facts**. Use [GPM IMERG daily rain](https://disc.gsfc.nasa.gov/datasets/GPM_3IMERGDF_07/summary) and [NASA POWER daily weather](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) as candidate weather inputs. Compute ETo by a documented FAO Penman–Monteith method from needed weather variables, or use a checked ETo input; do not treat reference ETo as observed field water use. FAO recommends daily data for AquaCrop calibration and warns that monthly or ten-day interpolated data are approximations. Compare NASA rain and weather against BMKG stations in the pilot before using them for numerical scenarios. [FAO ETo Calculator](https://www.fao.org/land-water/resources/tools/software/eto-calculator/en) · [FAO AquaCrop calibration guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/i2800e/i2800e.pdf) · [BMKG station data service](https://dataonline.bmkg.go.id/faq)

## Soil health and rotation effects

Kementan's research on irrigated paddy fields reports that rice–secondary-crop rotations can affect soil structure and chemistry, but the direction and size depend on the management and place. A legume in a sequence is therefore a **practice indicator**, not proof that nitrogen or organic carbon rose in this Java field. [Kementan rotation study](https://repository.pertanian.go.id/handle/123456789/8163)

AquaCrop 7.3 lets a user set soil fertility stress and calibrate a crop's response to it. It does **not** turn a proposed rotation into a measured change in organic carbon, nutrients, biological activity, or soil health. Its 7.3 crop annex states that crop parameters were tested largely with good soil mineral nutrient status, and that the fertility feature is still being tested. Its multi-run carry-over explicitly covers soil **water and salinity**. Thus, do not let a rice–soybean run silently create a nitrogen credit in the next rice season. Any such credit needs a separate, local measured or independently validated nutrient model. [FAO 7.3 user guide, sections 2.10.12, 2.13.1 and 2.17](https://www.fao.org/docs/aquacroplibraries/default-document-library/chapter2aquacropv73.pdf) · [FAO 7.3 crop annex, introduction](https://www.fao.org/docs/aquacroplibraries/default-document-library/annexesaquacropv73.pdf)

Show soil outcomes in three distinct forms: **measured baseline** (dated soil samples and method); **practice-related hypothesis** (such as adding a legume or a dry season); and **measured change** only after repeat sampling under a consistent design. Do not rank candidate plans by an invented soil-health score. Record pH, organic carbon, nutrients, bulk density or infiltration only where the pilot can measure them, with local experts selecting the final indicators. This is a proposed evidence rule derived from the Kementan findings and AquaCrop's limits. [Kementan rotation study](https://repository.pertanian.go.id/handle/123456789/8163) · [FAO 7.3 user guide](https://www.fao.org/docs/aquacroplibraries/default-document-library/chapter2aquacropv73.pdf)

## Pilot calibration and validation gate

1. **Define cases with an agronomist:** at least one rainfed and one irrigated farming-zone type if both occur locally. Choose observed rice–rice and rice–secondary-crop sequences only where records exist. The exact number of zones and crops depends on the chosen pilot.
2. **Assemble independent field records:** planting and harvest dates, cultivar, grain yield with moisture basis, irrigation volume or application dates, soil layers and water properties, and repeat observations of canopy or soil water. Verify rain and temperature against BMKG stations. FAO lists these as model inputs and recommends testing crop simulations against observed canopy, soil water, biomass and yield. [FAO input requirements](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/input-requirements/) · [FAO 7.3 user guide, section 2.22](https://www.fao.org/docs/aquacroplibraries/default-document-library/chapter2aquacropv73.pdf)
3. **Calibrate on some seasons and fields; hold out others.** Check the calendar and water baseline against actual planting feasibility and irrigation records. For AquaCrop, check simulated crop timing, canopy, soil water, water use, and yield against held-out observations. Report error by crop and field type, plus which plan rankings change when rain, irrigation, or soil inputs vary. FAO's calibration guide calls for local validation even where species parameters exist. [FAO calibration guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/i2800e/i2800e.pdf)
4. **Compare with KATAM:** note when the tool gives the same planting pattern and when it changes the choice; explain the new water or soil evidence behind any difference. If the baseline or model cannot beat a simple KATAM-informed choice on a decision the agency cares about, keep the output as an exploratory analysis. [Kementan KATAM](https://repository.pertanian.go.id/handle/123456789/22624)

**Release rule proposed for the pilot:** publish feasibility and weather exposure once their inputs are checked; label crop-water numbers as estimates; publish AquaCrop yield and water-productivity *scenarios* only after local held-out validation. Never label simulated future yield or soil health as an observed result. Do not scale a model calibrated in one pilot to all Java without testing the new field types and climate zones. This rule is an inference from FAO's model scope and calibration guidance. [FAO model limits](https://www.fao.org/aquacrop/overview/en) · [FAO calibration guidance](https://www.fao.org/4/i2800e/i2800e.pdf)
