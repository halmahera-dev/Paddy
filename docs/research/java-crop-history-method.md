# Reconstructing crop history in a Java pilot

**Checked:** 26 September 2026. **Decision:** use dated local field records as the source of crop names and sequence, and use NASA HLS plus Sentinel-1 to check timing and fill limited gaps. Do not turn a vegetation signal into a confirmed crop name. This is a proposed pilot method, not a tested model.

## Why this is possible, and where it stops

NASA's Harmonized Landsat–Sentinel-2 (HLS) V2 supplies surface reflectance on a common 30 m grid. Landsat input begins in 2013 and Sentinel-2 input in 2015. Its quality layer marks cloud, cloud shadow, adjacent cloud or shadow, water, and aerosol level. This makes HLS a useful historical record of green-up, senescence, and exposed or flooded land **where pixels and clear views exist**. A 30 m pixel covers 900 m²; small or narrow plots can share pixels with other fields. HLS cannot directly measure crop species, planting, harvest, or legal field ownership. [NASA HLS V2 guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf)

There is direct Java evidence for the value and limit of satellite inference. A [West Java study by Thorp and Drajat](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/57795/Thorp2021%20-%20rice%20Indonesia.pdf) trained on monthly BPS survey observations at 21,696 locations from November 2018 to April 2019. Its best five-class rice-stage model reached **75.9% test accuracy** with Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 optical data, versus 69.1% with radar alone and 72.8% with optical alone. The authors report substantial cloud loss. They also **merged other crops and non-agricultural land into one non-rice class** because the classes were often confused. These are that study's results, not expected accuracy for this pilot or for HLS. They support a narrow first target: rice cycle versus verified named non-rice crop versus unknown, with independent pilot validation. [West Java study](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/57795/Thorp2021%20-%20rice%20Indonesia.pdf)

The same study reports a main West Java rice season from about October/November to March/April and a second irrigated season from about March/April to August/September; field sizes were typically under 1 ha in its study region. Do **not** force all fields into these two windows. Trace actual planting and harvest events on a continuous calendar, because an irrigated field may have two or more crops within a year, while a rainfed field may not. [West Java study, study region](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/57795/Thorp2021%20-%20rice%20Indonesia.pdf)

## Evidence sources and their roles

| Source | What it may establish | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Partner field records and farmer/extension officer checks** | A named crop, approximate planting and harvest dates, variety if known, irrigation status, and an identified plot. Ask for two or more complete years where available. | Records may be incomplete, recalled late, or keyed to a farmer rather than a stable plot. Verify a sample against photos, logs, or field visits. |
| **BPS KSA survey** | BPS records rice growth stage and other field states at sampled points each month. Its method is direct observation used to estimate areas. In the West Java study, BPS supplied georeferenced observations with photos. | It is a **sample survey**, not a field census or a public parcel-level crop-history layer. Access to georeferenced records needs a BPS agreement; published totals alone cannot label a plot. BPS's “other crop” class does not name maize or soybean. [BPS method](https://www.bps.go.id/id/publication/2018/12/21/5aaf63e44aa5e46e815a1e8f/upaya-perbaikan-data-padi-dengan-metode-kerangka-sampel-area-ksa-2018.html) · [West Java study](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/57795/Thorp2021%20-%20rice%20Indonesia.pdf) |
| **Kementan SIMOTANDI** | A Sentinel-1-based rice-phase map and area totals down to *kecamatan*. Kementan says its model uses field checks and local reports. Use as a rice timing and area comparison, if historic pilot data can be obtained. | Its public table is area by phase; “fallow and non-rice” is one group. It does not identify a named alternative crop or each field's sequence. It is itself a satellite model, so it is **not independent ground truth**. [Method](https://simotandi.pertanian.go.id/front-tentang-kami) · [public table](https://simotandi.pertanian.go.id/data-tabular) |
| **NASA HLS V2** | Optical vegetation and water-related time series, with per-observation quality flags. Use it first for crop-cycle evidence. | Cloud gaps and mixed 30 m pixels; no direct species label. [HLS guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf) |
| **Copernicus Sentinel-1** | Radar backscatter through cloud, useful to check flooded rice preparation and crop growth where HLS has gaps. ESA documents the value of radar for wet rice landscapes. | Water and crop structure signals are indirect. Processing and local training are needed; it cannot reliably name all secondary crops by itself. [ESA rice example](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1/10_ways_Sentinel-1_data_lets_us_%E2%80%98see%E2%80%99_our_world) · [West Java study](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/57795/Thorp2021%20-%20rice%20Indonesia.pdf) |
| **FTW field polygons** | Candidate map units for sample selection and image extraction in 2024–25. | Predicted remote-sensing units, not legal parcels or stable historic IDs. Check each pilot boundary against a local map or GPS visit before attaching a multi-year sequence. [FTW data notes](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data) |

The local partner should first check whether plot-linked cropping logs exist. Kementan's e-RDKK contains planned commodity and area data, but a **plan is not proof of what grew or was harvested**. Use it to find contacts or candidate records only after a data agreement, and protect personal data. [Kementan e-RDKK description](https://psp.pertanian.go.id/berita/mentan-amran-sebut-digitalisasi-e-rdkk-guna-pastikan-pupuk-subsidi-tepat-sasaran)

## A pilot workflow

1. **Choose a tractable area and seasons.** Select one district or farming zone with a partner that can provide at least two years of dated crop and irrigation records, plus visits during a new season. Include irrigated and rainfed plots, small and larger plots, and rice and known secondary crops. This selection is a recommendation based on the evidence gaps above.
2. **Build a plot and event register.** Record a local plot ID and verified geometry; crop name, planting and harvest date range for each cycle; source document or person; date recorded; and any dispute. Keep separate event rows so `rice → rice → maize` and `rice → fallow → rice` remain distinct. Link FTW polygons only after checking overlap and splits. [FTW caveats](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data)
3. **Inspect image fitness before classifying.** For each plot and crop cycle, count HLS pixels wholly inside the verified boundary and clear observations through preparation, growth, and harvest. Apply the HLS QA bits for cloud, shadow, and adjacency; save the clear-view count and longest gap. Mark plots with no reliable interior pixel or key-stage views as unsuitable for HLS-only inference. Do not fill a long monsoon gap with a smooth curve and call it observed. [HLS guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf)
4. **Add radar and extract candidate cycles.** Use Sentinel-1 time series to help locate flooding or preparation and growth changes during HLS gaps. Combine it with HLS vegetation and water indices to estimate candidate start and end windows. Rice can have a flood-to-growth signal, but a cycle signal alone is not a species label. Compare candidate cycles with SIMOTANDI's rice-phase timing at the appropriate area scale. [ESA rice monitoring](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1/Sentinel-1_sees_rice_paddy_drop_in_the_Mekong_Delta) · [Kementan SIMOTANDI](https://simotandi.pertanian.go.id/front-tentang-kami)
5. **Train only classes that have labels.** Start with rice, a single named local alternative crop **only if** partner records cover enough dated examples, fallow/bare, and unknown. Treat BPS “other crop” and SIMOTANDI “non-rice” as broad comparison classes, never as maize or soybean labels. Prefer simple, inspectable seasonal features and a small classifier before a deep model; the data and validation should decide the model. [BPS-based West Java class definitions and results](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/57795/Thorp2021%20-%20rice%20Indonesia.pdf)
6. **Validate independently.** Hold out whole fields and, if enough data exist, another season or village; do not put repeated observations of one plot in both training and test sets. Check held-out crop names and event dates with field logs, geotagged photos, farmer and officer interviews, and new visits. Report a confusion matrix by crop, missed and false rice cycles, date error, and a breakdown by plot size, irrigation, and optical clear-view count. Compare area totals with BPS and SIMOTANDI only as a separate area check. These are proposed evaluation controls, not measured performance claims. [BPS KSA method](https://www.bps.go.id/id/publication/2018/12/21/5aaf63e44aa5e46e815a1e8f/upaya-perbaikan-data-padi-dengan-metode-kerangka-sampel-area-ksa-2018.html) · [West Java study](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/57795/Thorp2021%20-%20rice%20Indonesia.pdf)

## What the first proof may say

- **Observed:** “Rice was planted here in season A,” only when a dated, plot-linked local record or direct check supports it. Show the source and date.
- **Inferred:** “Satellite pattern is consistent with a rice cycle between these dates,” when a validated local model, adequate image coverage, and checked geometry support it. Show a confidence or quality band and the key observations. A named non-rice crop may be inferred only after that class passes independent pilot validation.
- **Unknown:** Use when a plot has no reliable record; geometry or pixels are too small or mixed; cloud and radar gaps leave stages unseen; or the model cannot separate rice, a secondary crop, and fallow. Do not silently turn “non-rice” into “maize,” “soybean,” or “fallow.”

**Decision gate:** If the partner cannot supply plot-linked crop events and an independent check sample, the pilot can show **area-level rice timing and uncertain crop-cover cycles**, but cannot claim a verified field rotation history. If named alternative crops cannot be validated, keep them unknown and defer crop-specific rotation advice. This is an inference from the source labels and image limits above.
