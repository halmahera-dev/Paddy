# Agriculture in Indonesia and data for a Java rotation tool

**Checked:** 26 September 2026. **Purpose:** inform product decisions for a long-term service used first by researchers or public agencies. The planned map covers Java; field-level rotation advice needs a pilot area and local checks.

## What the current evidence says

- Indonesia harvested **11.32 million hectares of paddy in 2025** and produced **60.21 million tonnes of dry unhusked paddy**. Harvested area rose 12.69% from 2024. This is *harvested area*, so a plot harvested twice can enter the total twice; it is not a map of unique fields. [BPS final 2025 rice release](https://www.bps.go.id/en/publication/2026/08/03/b40248f696f16d51371d326e/luas-panen-dan-produksi-padi-di-indonesia-2025.html)
- Indonesia harvested **2.71 million hectares of shelled maize in 2025** and produced **16.16 million tonnes** of dry shelled maize at 14% moisture. This shows that rice and maize both merit consideration, but national totals do not show which crops can follow one another on a given field. [BPS final 2025 maize release](https://www.bps.go.id/en/pressrelease/2026/02/02/2538/luas-panen-jagung-pipilan-pada-2025-sebesar-2-71-juta-hektare--mengalami-kenaikan-sebesar-0-16-juta-hektare-atau-6-35-persen.html)
- The **2023 agricultural census** counted 25.12 million agricultural business households and 17.25 million farmers using less than 0.5 hectare. In the census table, West Java, Central Java and East Java alone account for about **10.50 million** of these smallholders (sum of BPS province figures). A design based only on large, clean satellite polygons would miss an important user setting. [BPS census summary](https://sensus.bps.go.id/main/index/st2023) · [BPS province table](https://sensus.bps.go.id/topik/tabular/st2023/215)
- Java is a strong initial region. In BPS's **2025** province table, East Java produced **10.44 million tonnes** of paddy, West Java **10.23 million**, and Central Java **9.30 million**. Together these three provinces produced about **49.8%** of Indonesia's 60.21 million tonnes. This is paddy production, not unique field area, and it excludes the other Java provinces. [BPS 2025 province table](https://www.bps.go.id/id/statistics-table/3/ZDNaak0yODBUVTlGYW5sa2REUkVUVVY1YVZkbmR6MDkjMw%3D%3D/produksi-padisup1-sup-dan-beras-menurut-provinsi--2022.html) · [BPS 2025 final release](https://www.bps.go.id/en/publication/2026/08/03/b40248f696f16d51371d326e/luas-panen-dan-produksi-padi-di-indonesia-2025.html)
- Weather risk affects current planting decisions. BMKG predicted that **64.5% of its Indonesian season zones** would have below-normal rain in the **2026 dry season**, and that **57.2%** would have a longer dry season than normal. These are national forecast shares by season zone, not measurements of Java's fields or observed crop losses. BMKG publishes local season forecasts, rain analysis, drought warnings, and plant water availability that need regular review for a pilot. [BMKG 2026 dry-season forecast](https://www.bmkg.go.id/iklim/prediksi-musim/prediksi-musim-kemarau-tahun-2026-di-indonesia) · [BMKG season forecast index](https://www.bmkg.go.id/iklim/prediksi-musim)
- Irrigation changes which rotations are feasible. A Kementan report from Malang names rice–rice–rice and rice–rice–secondary crop as patterns where irrigation is available almost year-round. A West Java field report says a rainfed area normally grew a secondary crop in the second season, then gained a second rice crop after pumps were installed. These are examples, not Java-wide rules. [Kementan Malang study](https://tanamanpangan.brmp.pertanian.go.id/informasi-publik/download/teknologi-budi-daya/evaluasi-kesuburan-lahan-dan-optimasi-pengelolaannya-di-ip2sip-kendalpayak) · [Kementan Tasikmalaya report](https://jabar.brmp.pertanian.go.id/berita/lahan-sawah-tadah-hujan-tasikmalaya-berhasil-diairi-petani-bisa-panen-dua-kali)

**Product inference:** Compare plausible rice and secondary-crop sequences under rainfed and irrigated conditions. Do not treat a satellite rain value as proof that irrigation water is available. Ask for or obtain water source, crop history, and local soil observations in the pilot.

## NASA data to use first

The grid size is the **source data scale**, even when the interface shows a small field. A value copied onto each field inside one grid cell does not become a separate field measurement.

| NASA source | Measures and access | Scale and fit | Limit for a Java field |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **GPM IMERG** | Estimated precipitation. GES DISC offers daily, half-hourly and monthly products through Earthdata Search, Giovanni, and data services. Use daily or ten-day totals, dry spells, and past seasonal variation for a region. [GES DISC daily product](https://disc.gsfc.nasa.gov/datasets/GPM_3IMERGDF_07/summary?keywords=imerg) | **0.1°**, about 10 km, daily or half-hourly. Good for a Java-wide rain layer and district or pilot-area history. [GES DISC](https://disc.gsfc.nasa.gov/datasets/GPM_3IMERGDF_07/summary?keywords=imerg) | It is a satellite estimate, not field rain or irrigation. NASA notes lower skill near coasts and complex terrain. **Version 07 Final currently ends at 30 September 2025**; NASA's August 2026 update says V08 Final was still pending. For recent monitoring, check the current Early/Late products and label their run and latency. Do not silently join them to Final as one uniform trend series. [GES DISC V07 record](https://disc.gsfc.nasa.gov/datasets/GPM_3IMERGDF_07/summary?keywords=imerg) · [NASA V08 update](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/news/update-imerg-v08-transition-schedule-aug-2026) |
| **NASA POWER** | Daily agroclimate API for air temperature, radiation, humidity, wind and precipitation. Useful to derive broad heat and water-demand indicators or feed a crop model. [POWER daily API](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/services/api/temporal/daily/) | Native meteorology is **0.5° × 0.625°** and primary solar data **1° × 1°**; the API offers daily time series. POWER can expose higher-resolution IMERG-derived rain, so inspect each parameter's source and grid. [POWER source methods](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources/) | Much too coarse for Java field microclimate. NASA says recent meteorological values mix near-real-time GEOS-IT with later MERRA-2 updates, so avoid treating the newest values as a fixed climate record. [POWER source methods](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/methodology/data/sources/) |
| **Harmonized Landsat–Sentinel-2 (HLS) v2** | Harmonized surface reflectance and quality mask for vegetation and land-cover time series. Access HLSL30 and HLSS30 in Earthdata Search, AppEEARS or cloud COG/STAC workflows. [NASA HLS guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf) · [NASA LP DAAC tutorial](https://git.earthdata.nasa.gov/projects/LPDUR/repos/hls-tutorial/commits/e0bed93b067fa22d06c38aa2d119149bd05a5693) | **30 m**, nominal combined observations every **2–3 days** globally. Best NASA layer here for pilot field vegetation time series and broad planting or harvest signals. [NASA LP DAAC tutorial](https://git.earthdata.nasa.gov/projects/LPDUR/repos/hls-tutorial/commits/e0bed93b067fa22d06c38aa2d119149bd05a5693) | Cloud-free observations can be much less frequent. Small plots can have few or mixed pixels. Vegetation signal alone does not prove crop species or a past rotation. A crop classifier needs local labels and validation. [NASA HLS guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf) |
| **SMAP Level 4, SPL4SMGP v8** | Model-assisted surface and root-zone soil moisture, with SMAP brightness temperatures assimilated into a land model. Download by Earthdata Login and subset with NASA tools. [NASA NSIDC product](https://nsidc.org/data/spl4smgp/versions/8) | **9 km**, **3-hourly**, March 2015 onward. Useful for area-level wetness anomalies and checking drought context. [NASA NSIDC product](https://nsidc.org/data/spl4smgp/versions/8) | It is not a field soil sample, nutrient measure, or water supply record. NASA reports a **14 May–28 July 2026 geolocation issue** affecting SMAP products and ongoing standard-product reprocessing; check data status before using that interval. [NASA NSIDC notice](https://nsidc.org/data/spl4smgp/versions/8) |
| **ECOSTRESS global v2** | Evapotranspiration and evaporative stress from the ISS sensor, via Earthdata Search. Use selected cloud-free scenes to investigate heat or water stress in a pilot. [NASA Earthdata collection list](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/site/collections/directory/LPCLOUD/gov.nasa.eosdis) | Global gridded ET and stress products are **70 m**. They can show more local detail than SMAP when a usable scene exists. [NASA Earthdata collection list](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/site/collections/directory/LPCLOUD/gov.nasa.eosdis) | ISS overpasses and clouds give irregular coverage. Do **not** use the separate 70 m *disALEXI daily* products for Java: NASA says those products cover the contiguous United States only. [NASA LP DAAC notice](https://forum.earthdata.nasa.gov/viewtopic.php?t=7252) |
| **NEX-GDDP-CMIP6** | Downscaled future **scenarios** for rainfall, maximum and minimum air temperature, humidity, radiation and wind; NASA provides netCDF subsets. Use multi-model ranges for long-term climate stress tests. [NASA NCCS data page](https://nccs.smce.nasa.gov/data-collections/nex-gddp-cmip6/) | **0.25°**, about 25 km, **daily** projections through 2100. Suitable for regional scenarios, not next-season weather. [NASA NCCS data page](https://nccs.smce.nasa.gov/data-collections/nex-gddp-cmip6/) | A future scenario is not a farm forecast. Compare several models and scenarios, and keep the outcome separate from observed NASA/BMKG weather. [NASA technical note](https://www.nccs.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/NEX-GDDP-CMIP6-v2-Tech_Note.pdf) |

**NASA-first sequence:** start with IMERG historical rain, POWER temperature/radiation, and HLS crop-cover time series. Add SMAP for area-level moisture context. Use ECOSTRESS when pilot scenes are adequate. Use NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 only for a clearly marked long-term scenario view. This is a proposed product sequence, not a validated agronomic model.

## FTW field boundaries: feasible map, limited advice

The **2026 Global Fields of the World** release contains estimated field polygons for **2024 and 2025**, plus 500 m confidence and quality layers. It offers GeoParquet for analysis and PMTiles for web maps under CC BY 4.0. The dataset includes an Indonesia country partition: an HTTP HEAD check on **26 September 2026** returned status 200 for [Indonesia.parquet](https://data.source.coop/ftw/global-data/predictions/vectors/alpha/results-by-admin-conf/admin:country_code=ID/Indonesia.parquet), with content length **3,917,438,214 bytes**. A browser should use PMTiles or a small server-side subset, not download that whole partition. [FTW catalog](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data) · [FTW vector collection](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data/predictions/vectors)

FTW defines a field as a predicted remote-sensing unit, **not a legal parcel**. Its authors warn that real fields in smallholder systems may get low confidence and that one legal parcel can map to several polygons or none. Its reported benchmark result across 24 countries is not a measured accuracy for Java; Java quality needs local comparison with known boundaries. The 2024 and 2025 polygons are independent predictions and must not be treated as stable field IDs without matching and checking. FTW supplies boundaries and confidence, **not verified crop species, crop history, soil chemistry, ownership, or a rotation recommendation**. [FTW catalog and caveats](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data)

## Product implications and research gaps

1. **Java-wide exploration is feasible in principle.** FTW PMTiles and NASA regional grids can support a map of predicted fields and rain, heat, wetness or vegetation context. This is an inference from dataset access and scale, not a tested Java deployment. [FTW catalog](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data) · [NASA GPM](https://disc.gsfc.nasa.gov/datasets/GPM_3IMERGDF_07/summary?keywords=imerg) · [NASA HLS guide](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1698/HLS_User_Guide_V2.pdf)
2. **Field rotation advice is a separate validation task.** The pilot needs verified field geometry, at least two seasons of crop history, irrigation access, local soil measurements, crop variety and timing, and outcomes that can be checked. SoilGrids and NASA grids can provide context but cannot fill all those facts. This is an inference from the source resolutions and variables above. [FTW caveats](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data) · [NASA SMAP](https://nsidc.org/data/spl4smgp/versions/8) · [ISRIC SoilGrids](https://docs.isric.org/globaldata/soilgrids/)
3. **Define the gain over KATAM before building advice.** Compare the pilot output with KATAM's existing planting and rotation guidance, and ask agency users which decision is still hard: comparing water demand, soil trade-offs, climate scenarios, or prioritising areas for field visits. This is a proposed research test. [Kementan KATAM-SC](https://jatim.brmp.pertanian.go.id/layanan/layanan-lainnya/kalender-tanam-terpadu-katam-terpadu-sc)
4. **Choose the pilot by partner data availability**, not by apparent FTW polygon density alone. The first site needs a willing local agency or research team, known field and irrigation records, and permission to check sample fields and soil. This is a recommendation derived from the data gaps above.
